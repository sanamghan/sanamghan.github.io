An Email System That Sounds Like You

Build an email brain first. Then let AI create drafts you review and send yourself.

30-MINUTE SETUPDRAFTS ONLYFULL WALKTHROUGH

What this does.

This system reviews the email you choose, prepares replies in your voice, and separately flags messages that need your judgment. It is designed to shorten the first-draft work, not to make commitments or send messages for you.

Step 1: Build your Email HQ.

Create one project or workspace for email. Add your role, company context, frequent contacts, preferred sign-off, and the specific ways your tone changes for clients, partners, and your team.

You are drafting email responses for [YOUR NAME].

Role: [TITLE AND RESPONSIBILITIES]
Company: [WHAT YOU DO]
Team: [KEY PEOPLE AND THEIR ROLES]

Tone rules:
Default tone: [TONE]
Clients: [TONE]
Internal team: [TONE]
Always sign off: [SIGN-OFF]

Important context: [RECURRING PRIORITIES, BOUNDARIES, OR CURRENT PROJECTS]

Step 2: Test it manually.

Paste three to five representative emails into the project before connecting any inbox. Ask for concise drafts. Notice every correction you make: words you would never use, context it missed, people who need a warmer or firmer tone. Put those corrections back into the instructions.

Step 3: Set the draft workflow.

Choose a cadence that matches your day. A morning run works well when your team has sent messages overnight. Exclude newsletters, automated notifications, CC-only messages, and anything that does not require a response.

Review emails received in the last [TIME WINDOW] that I have not responded to. Draft a concise response in my voice using the Email HQ context.

For each draft, show: sender, subject, draft response.

Skip newsletters, automated notifications, CC-only emails, and messages that do not need a reply. Put anything that needs a decision, missing context, or careful review under “Needs Your Input.”

Step 4: Review your new morning.

Open the drafts. Check names, dates, commitments, attachments, and tone. Edit anything that requires your personal context. Send only when you are comfortable with the message.

Improve it over time.

In the first week, expect to edit a lot. Each repeated correction belongs in Email HQ. By the second or third week, the drafts should improve because the instructions are sharper. Update the context whenever your team, clients, priorities, or sign-off change.

Keep this human

Never auto-send. Personally review legal, financial, HR, sensitive client, board, or crisis communication. AI cannot know a decision made in a room it was not in.

Your next move

Test five drafts manually today. Do not automate until the manual output actually sounds like you.
