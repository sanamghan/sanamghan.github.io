5 Stupid-Easy AI Automations You Can Set Up With Claude

Copy a prompt, fill in the brackets, and schedule it in Claude. Start with the output you will actually read.

Before you start

  1. Download the Claude desktop app and turn on CoWork. These workflows are built for Claude’s desktop experience, so start there rather than trying to force them through a browser tab.
  2. Connect the tools you want Claude to reference. In the top left, go to Customize, then Connectors. Add the tools that make sense for you, like your calendar, email, or Slack. Only connect what you are comfortable giving Claude access to.
  3. Open a new chat, switch on CoWork, and pick one workflow. Do not set up all five at once. Start with the one that would genuinely make your week easier, test the output, and adjust it before you add another.

1. Morning priorities rundown

Stop checking five apps to figure out your day. Have Claude review your calendar, unread email, and Slack mentions each morning, then tell you what actually needs attention.

Every weekday at [TIME], review my calendar, unread email, and Slack mentions. Give me today’s meetings, my top three priorities, and anything waiting on me for over 48 hours. Send the summary to [WHERE].

Customize: time, destination.

2. Meeting prep

Walk into meetings already caught up. Get a briefing on who you are meeting, what you last discussed, and what needs following up.

Every weekday at [TIME], look at tomorrow’s calendar. For each meeting, review recent emails and Slack messages with the attendees. Summarize who is on the call, what we last discussed, and any open questions. Send it to [WHERE].

Customize: time, destination.

3. Follow-up chaser

Stop letting good conversations disappear into your inbox. Have Claude find unanswered emails and prepare follow-up drafts for your review.

Every [DAY] at [TIME], find emails I sent in the past [NUMBER] days that have not received a reply. List the recipient, subject, and number of days outstanding. Draft a short follow-up for each. Send the list to [WHERE]. Do not send any emails.

Customize: day, time, number of days, destination.

4. End-of-day wrap

Close your laptop without forgetting what you promised. Review commitments from the day and identify what deserves attention tomorrow.

Every weekday at [TIME], review the emails I sent and Slack messages I wrote today. List what I committed to, what’s still open, and the first thing I should do tomorrow. Send it to [WHERE].

Customize: time, destination.

5. Weekly wins recap

Your Friday reminder that you actually did things this week. Have Claude collect wins, milestones, and positive feedback that would otherwise get lost.

Every Friday at [TIME], review Slack messages and emails from the past week for wins, milestones, and positive feedback. List each win with relevant context and numbers where available. Send the recap to [WHERE].

Customize: time, destination.

Before you automate anything

An automation is only useful if you actually use the output. Ask yourself: Will I genuinely read this? Where should the output land? How often do I need it?

Set it up safely.

Test one automation manually before you schedule it. Confirm that Claude can access the sources you expect, that the destination is correct, and that the output is short enough to be useful. Keep follow-ups as drafts. Review names, dates, commitments, and anything that could affect another person before you act on it.

Your next move

Pick one useful automation. Test it, refine it, and only then add more.
