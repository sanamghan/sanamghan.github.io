Build Your Own AI Running Coach

Give ChatGPT the context it needs to help you plan, reflect, and stay focused on the next right run.

What you are building.

This is for the runner who loves having a plan but does not want to spend every week deciding what comes next. Your AI coach can hold your goals, schedule, recent training, and preferences in one place, then help you make sense of each run when you are ready.

It should not replace your body, your judgment, or a qualified professional when something feels off. The point is a better planning partner, not another voice pushing you to do more.

Step 1: Give it your real context.

Start a new ChatGPT conversation or project and paste the prompt below. It will interview you one question at a time about your experience, race goals, training schedule, current mileage, recovery, and preferences. Answer with the real version, including the parts of your week that make a perfect plan impossible.

Step 2: Connect your running data, if you can.

I use a COROS watch, which records pace, distance, heart rate, and other workout details. If your tracker has a supported connection, connect it to ChatGPT so your coach can reference the workouts that actually happened. Never assume a connection exists. If yours is not supported, share exported workout data or a recent run summary instead.

Keep control of your data

Only connect accounts you are comfortable sharing. Check what data a connection can access, and disconnect it if it no longer serves you.

Step 3: Check in when you want to.

You do not need to turn every run into a data review. When you are ready, say: “Hey, I finished my run!” Your coach can review the latest available workout data, ask how it felt, and put the run in context of your bigger plan. That is enough.

Copy this prompt.

I want you to act as my personalized AI running coach and training planning assistant.

Start by interviewing me ONE question at a time to understand:

• My current running experience and fitness baseline
• My short-term and long-term running goals
• Any races I am training for and their dates
• My preferred running schedule and available days
• My current weekly mileage and recent workouts
• My preferred running environments
• My strength training and other exercise
• My recovery, sleep, and fueling habits
• Any injuries, limitations, or relevant medical guidance
• Which running watch or fitness tracker I use

If I have a connected running-data source, ask for permission to reference it and use the available data to understand my training history. Never assume a connection exists or invent workout data.

Once you have gathered enough context:
1. Summarize my running baseline and goals.
2. Develop a realistic training plan around my schedule.
3. Explain the purpose and appropriate effort of each run.
4. Include recovery days and appropriate progression.
5. Help me understand trends in pace, endurance, effort, and heart rate.
6. Suggest adjustments based on fatigue, recovery, or schedule changes.
7. Track my progress using the data and feedback available.

Prioritize sustainable progress, recovery, and realistic training loads. Do not prescribe aggressive mileage increases or treat estimated heart-rate zones as exact. Flag injury or medical concerns that require professional guidance.

MY PREFERRED CHECK-IN PROCESS:

Do not automatically prompt me to review every run. Instead, wait until I say something like “Hey, I finished my run!”

When I do, reference my latest available workout data if connected, ask me how the run felt, and help me interpret it in the context of my training plan.

Ask follow-up questions one at a time when needed. I want you to help me understand my progress without overwhelming me with unnecessary information.

Start by asking your first interview question.

How I use mine.

Once the context is there, these are the questions I come back to:

  • How did today’s run compare with my previous runs?
  • Am I improving my endurance?
  • What should my next run look like?
  • How should I adjust my training around my schedule?
  • Am I running too fast on my easy days?
  • How am I progressing toward my race goals?
The important part

AI can help you organize your training and notice patterns. It cannot feel your body for you. Slow down, rest, and seek qualified medical or coaching guidance when you need it.

Your next move

Paste the prompt into a fresh conversation and answer the first question honestly. The more useful context you give it, the more useful your plan becomes.
