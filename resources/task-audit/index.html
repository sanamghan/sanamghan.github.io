The AI Task Audit

Stop asking, “What can AI do?” Start with a more useful question: “Which part of my actual week deserves AI’s help?”

12 MINUTESWORKSHEET + COPYABLE PROMPTBEGINNER-FRIENDLY

AI works best when it has a clear, bounded job. This exercise turns the vague pressure to “use AI more” into three sensible experiments you can test this week.

The rule

Do not automate the part that requires your accountability, taste, relationship, or final judgment. Let AI support the repeatable parts around it.

01

List your real week.

Write down 10 tasks from the past seven days. Keep them concrete: “summarized customer interviews,” “sent follow-up emails,” or “prepared a Monday update.”

02

Sort each task.

Automate

Repeatable, low-risk, and easy to check. Example: collecting links for a weekly reading list.

AI assists

You still direct and decide, but AI helps you research, organize, compare, outline, or create a first pass.

Keep human

High-stakes, sensitive, relationship-based, or dependent on your lived judgment. AI may prepare materials, but it does not own the decision.

03

Pick one experiment.

Choose a task from the “AI assists” column. It should happen often enough to matter, be small enough to review in ten minutes, and have a clear definition of “good.”

I want to improve one recurring task with AI.

The task: [describe it]
What a good result looks like: [specific outcome]
What I need to keep control of: [judgment, approvals, sensitive details]
What information you can use: [safe inputs and sources]

First, ask me up to five questions to make the task clearer. Then propose a small, reviewable workflow, not a fully autonomous system. Show me the inputs, each AI step, the human check, and the final output.
Your next move

Run one experiment this week. If it works twice with a light review, document the workflow. If it doesn’t, that is useful information too.
