Turn Your Experience Into an AI-Enabled Offer

Your next offer may already be hiding in work you have done well. Use AI to identify the pattern, then validate the offer in the real world.

45 MINUTES3 DELIVERABLESOFFER + PRICING OPTIONS

What this exercise is for.

This is not a prompt for inventing a business from scratch. It is a way to look backward at work people already valued, find the high-value problem that connects it, and turn that evidence into a clearer service or offer.

Step 1: Pick three past deliverables.

Start on paper. Choose three pieces of work people thanked you for or that led to a meaningful result: a campaign recap, strategy deck, audit, launch plan, client system, or research project. Under each, write who it was for, the situation before you stepped in, what you actually did, and what changed after you handed it over.

Before you upload anything

Remove confidential client information, internal data, passwords, personal details, and anything you do not have permission to share.

Step 2: Add the work to a fresh AI conversation.

Upload all three deliverables, along with your notes from the first step. Do not ask for recommendations immediately. The quality comes from giving the model enough context about your role and the actual results.

Step 3: Run the interview first.

You are a business strategist who helps people package their experience into paid offers.

I am sharing three past deliverables. Before you analyze them, ask me five questions, one at a time, about the context, my role, what I was responsible for, and the results.

After I answer, do the following:
1. Name the high-value problem I solved in each deliverable.
2. Identify the hard and soft skills that connect all three.
3. Turn those patterns into three productized offers. For each offer, include who it is for, what is included, the intended outcome, and a price range to validate.
4. Show the same offer as hourly, project-based, and retainer work.

End with the one offer I should lead with and explain why. Do not invent results, testimonials, credentials, demand, or pricing facts.

Step 4: Answer the questions with real detail.

Let the interview happen one question at a time. Your answers matter more than the uploaded files. Explain the messy middle: constraints, decisions you made, what the client or team actually needed, and the changes you can honestly support.

Step 5: Read the output as a hypothesis.

You should receive a problem statement for each project, recurring skills, three potential offers, and three ways to price each one. This is a starting point, not a final business plan. Check whether the language sounds like your work and whether you would want to deliver the offer repeatedly.

Step 6: Choose the offer to test.

Lead with the offer that has the clearest buyer, the clearest outcome, and the strongest evidence from your past work. Test it in a real conversation with someone in the target audience. Listen for the language they use to describe the problem, then revise the offer around that evidence.

Keep this human

AI can surface patterns across your work. It cannot prove that an offer has demand, set a market price, or make claims you cannot substantiate. You own the validation.

Your next move

Pick the three deliverables today. Do the interview before you spend time naming, branding, or building anything.
